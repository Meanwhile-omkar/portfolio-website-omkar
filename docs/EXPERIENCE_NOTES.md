## Infiheal: what I liked most

> I was directly working with the founder, so whenever there were pitches or decision-making scenarios, I was the youngest in the room, and my opinions and ideas were respected and heard, and some were implemented too. I feel really proud of that; I got such a big opportunity. I've had meetings with a lot of international delegates, and I attended exclusive tech fests and conferences representing Infiheal. It gave me very good opportunities at a very young age.

## Bigul: what I liked most

> I reported to my manager weekly. The meeting was scheduled for 15–30 minutes, but sometimes it went on for 2 hours, because my analysis was deep and accurate, with pictures and hypotheses, and my manager (from IIM) would get hooked. One day he said he trusts me more than his IT team. At the end of my internship he wrote my recommendation letter himself, not with AI.

## CypherSOL: what I liked most

> My first offline, in-office internship at a startup. I could see different departments working at the same time and understand how a startup runs. When you work remotely you only connect with the people you need to; in the office you can see and talk to anyone.
>
> The main constraint was money. We worked with financial agencies and CAs, so the whole product ran offline. Any ML or deep learning model had to work offline on constrained devices with limited resources: no cloud, no AI services. That was challenging, but it was fun to solve. My manager was very chill, and I learned a lot from him.

## Sereno Volante: what I liked most

> My first technical internship, where I was actually coding and building projects. It was high stakes, working with JSW. I ran the meetings, understood their problems and did the product reviews. I was the one building the project, so I led it and owned it completely: I explained it to them, took their opinions, and rebuilt it. That meant a lot of conversations with them. Dealing with such a big client in my first experience gave me a real kick.
>
> I also explored a lot of industrial requirements where AI could give real value, and got to understand different industries: automobile, steel, sales and more.

## IIT Bombay (KCDH): what I liked most

> My very first internship in data science. It was a research support internship: I helped with research that was already going on. Getting it was a real struggle. I emailed a lot of professors, very few replied, and then came a long trail of emails and meetings before I finally got it.
>
> I worked on a very niche topic and learned how research is done: understanding the problem statement, doing a literature review, working out what novelty you can add, forming and testing hypotheses, and noting and documenting everything.
